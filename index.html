01 / THE IDEA

Abstract

Alignment meets diversity.
Learn the regularization signal.

Diffusion models have demonstrated the effectiveness of score-based objectives for generative modeling, yet their potential for self-supervised representation learning remains underexplored. We introduce Entropy-Based Regularization (EBReg), a framework that uses latent score estimation to regularize the latent distribution during self-supervised training.

EBReg jointly learns an encoder and a latent score model, and utilizes the learned score to provide a diversity-preserving signal that prevents representational collapse. Motivated by a mutual-information lower bound, we formulate the objective as view alignment with smoothed marginal entropy maximization and optimize the marginal entropy without explicit density evaluation.

The same score model can additionally be used at test time to refine corrupted representations toward the training latent distribution. Empirically, EBReg learns competitive representations on ImageNet-1K, achieving 75.57% linear-probe accuracy with ViT-L. Across diverse architectures, score-guided test-time adaptation further improves robustness on ImageNet-C, yielding an average relative accuracy gain of approximately 5%.

GAUSSIAN SMOOTHING

A density beyond the subspace.

Adding Gaussian noise gives the embeddings a full-dimensional density, even when the clean representations occupy a lower-dimensional subspace. This makes the smoothed entropy well-defined.

Why smooth the distribution? ↗

PARAMETRIC SCORE LEARNING

A model that tracks the embeddings.

A noise-prediction network learns the score of the evolving, smoothed distribution. Its estimated score supplies entropy-promoting encoder updates and can be reused for test-time adaptation.

02 / THE METHOD

One score. Two roles.

Encourage diversity during training.
Recover structure at test time.

01

Align the views

A shared encoder and projector turn two augmented views into embeddings. An alignment loss brings paired representations together.

02

Learn the latent score

Add Gaussian noise to detached embeddings and fit a noise predictor. Alternate score fitting with encoder updates.

03

Preserve diversity

Hold the score fixed and update the encoder against its direction. A scale constraint prevents entropy from increasing through rescaling alone.

During training, move against the score to promote diversity. During adaptation, move toward higher source density using unlabeled test inputs. The score model stays frozen; only normalization parameters change.

CONNECTION TO EXISTING REGULARIZERS

Where does the diversity signal come from?

Contrastive regularization and EBReg both encourage diversity alongside view alignment. A contrastive regularizer such as InfoNCE uses explicit negative pairs; EBReg obtains an entropy-promoting signal from a learned latent score.

Training pseudocodeTwo alternating updates

For each minibatch, fit the score model on detached embeddings, then update the encoder and projector using a detached score. Embeddings are normalized before adding noise; alignment uses the clean paired views.

Full algorithm in the paper ↗
# 1. Fit the score model; encoder and projector stay fixed.
h_1, h_2 = normalized_embeddings(view_1, view_2)
h_bar = stopgrad(concatenate(h_1, h_2))
repeat K_score times:
    eps = standard_gaussian_like(h_bar)
    update_score_model(MSE(predict_noise(h_bar + sigma * eps), eps))

# 2. Recompute embeddings with encoder gradients; detach the score.
h_1, h_2 = normalized_embeddings(view_1, view_2)
h = concatenate(h_1, h_2)
h_noisy = h + sigma * standard_gaussian_like(h)
score = stopgrad(-predict_noise(h_noisy) / sigma)
loss = mean_squared_alignment(h_1, h_2) + gamma * mean(dot(score, h_noisy))
update_encoder_and_projector(loss)

Training cost & scope

In the paper’s ViT-L comparison, EBReg adds a 5.21M-parameter score network. Training takes 500 seconds per epoch versus 440 seconds for SIGReg, with peak memory of 89.84 GB versus 90.33 GB on identical hardware (Table 4). The theoretical analysis of low-variance directions assumes an exact score and specific distributional conditions; the illustrative animations are not a guarantee of recovery from exact collapse.
